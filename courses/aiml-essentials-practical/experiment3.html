EXPERIMENT 3: Simple Bayesian Network

1. Aim

To construct a simple Bayesian Network, define Conditional Probability Tables (CPTs), and perform inference based on observed evidence.

2. Learning Objectives

After completing this practical, students will be able to:

  1. Understand the basic structure of a Bayesian Network.
  2. Represent relationships between variables using a simple directed graph.
  3. Understand and construct a Conditional Probability Table (CPT).
  4. Perform simple probabilistic inference using observed evidence.
  5. Understand how new evidence updates an initial belief.

3. Introduction

A Bayesian Network is a graphical representation of probabilistic relationships between different variables.

It consists of:

  • Nodes – represent variables.
  • Directed edges – represent relationships between variables.
  • Conditional Probability Tables (CPTs) – represent the probabilities associated with the variables.

In this practical, we will use three variables:

  • Cloudy
  • Rain Sprinkle
  • Wet Grass

The relationship can be represented as: 


        Cloudy
           |
           ↓
    Rain Sprinkle
           |
           ↓
       Wet Grass

Here:

  • Cloudy influences the probability of Rain Sprinkle.
  • Rain Sprinkle influences the probability of Wet Grass.

This practical follows the same Bayesian idea from the course material: an initial belief is updated when new evidence becomes available.

4. Bayesian Network Structure

Our simple Bayesian Network is: 


          Cloudy
             |
             ↓
      Rain Sprinkle
             |
             ↓
         Wet Grass

Each variable has two possible states.

Cloudy

  • Cloudy
  • Not Cloudy

Rain Sprinkle

  • Rain
  • No Rain

Wet Grass

  • Wet
  • Dry

5. Dataset

Suppose the following observations were recorded for 100 days:

Cloudy Rain Sprinkle Wet Grass Number of Days
Cloudy Rain Wet 40
Cloudy Rain Dry 5
Cloudy No Rain Wet 5
Cloudy No Rain Dry 10
Not Cloudy Rain Wet 5
Not Cloudy Rain Dry 5
Not Cloudy No Rain Wet 2
Not Cloudy No Rain Dry 28
Total 100

This dataset will be used to calculate the probabilities required for the Bayesian Network.

6. Step 1 – Find the Prior Probability

The prior probability represents our belief before observing new evidence.

Suppose we want to find the probability that it is cloudy.

From the data: 


Cloudy days = 40 + 5 + 5 + 10

             = 60 days

Total days = 100

Therefore, 


P(Cloudy) = 60 / 100

          = 0.60

Therefore: 


P(Cloudy) = 0.60 or 60%

Similarly, 


P(Not Cloudy) = 40 / 100

              = 0.40

The idea of prior probability follows the course material, where the prior represents our belief before observing the evidence.

7. Step 2 – Define the Conditional Probability Table (CPT)

We first create the CPT for Rain Sprinkle.

CPT for Rain Sprinkle

When it is Cloudy

There are 60 cloudy days.

Among these:

  • 45 days had rain.
  • 15 days had no rain.

Therefore, 


P(Rain | Cloudy) = 45 / 60

                 = 0.75


P(No Rain | Cloudy) = 15 / 60

                    = 0.25

When it is Not Cloudy

There are 40 non-cloudy days.

Among these:

  • 10 days had rain.
  • 30 days had no rain.

Therefore, 


P(Rain | Not Cloudy) = 10 / 40

                     = 0.25


P(No Rain | Not Cloudy) = 30 / 40

                        = 0.75

CPT for Rain Sprinkle

Cloudy Rain No Rain
Cloudy 0.75 0.25
Not Cloudy 0.25 0.75

Notice that each row adds up to 1: 


0.75 + 0.25 = 1

0.25 + 0.75 = 1

8. Step 3 – Define CPT for Wet Grass

Now we calculate the probability of Wet Grass based on whether there is rain.

When there is Rain

There are: 


40 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 55 rainy days

Among these, grass was wet on: 


40 + 5 + 5 = 50 days

Therefore, 


P(Wet Grass | Rain) = 50 / 55

                    ≈ 0.91

And: 


P(Dry Grass | Rain) = 5 / 55

                    ≈ 0.09

When there is No Rain

There are: 


5 + 10 + 2 + 28 = 45 days

Among these, grass was wet on: 


5 + 2 = 7 days

Therefore, 


P(Wet Grass | No Rain) = 7 / 45

                       ≈ 0.16

And: 


P(Dry Grass | No Rain) = 38 / 45

                       ≈ 0.84

CPT for Wet Grass

Rain Sprinkle Wet Grass Dry Grass
Rain 0.91 0.09
No Rain 0.16 0.84

Again, each row approximately adds up to 1.

9. Step 4 – Perform Inference

Suppose we observe the following evidence:

Evidence: It is Cloudy.

We want to find: 


P(Rain | Cloudy)

From the CPT: 


P(Rain | Cloudy) = 0.75

Therefore:

Probability of Rain given Cloudy = 75%

This means that after observing that the sky is cloudy, the probability of rain is 75%.

This demonstrates the basic Bayesian idea that new evidence can change our belief about an event.

10. Inference for Wet Grass

Now suppose we observe:

Evidence: It is raining.

We want to find: 


P(Wet Grass | Rain)

From the CPT: 


P(Wet Grass | Rain) ≈ 0.91

Therefore:

Probability of Wet Grass given Rain ≈ 91%

Thus, observing rain gives us a high probability that the grass will be wet.

11. Inference with No Rain

Now suppose the evidence is:

Evidence: There is no rain.

We want to find: 


P(Wet Grass | No Rain)

From the CPT: 


P(Wet Grass | No Rain) ≈ 0.16

Therefore:

Probability of Wet Grass given No Rain ≈ 16%

Thus, when there is no rain, the probability of wet grass is much lower.

12. Summary of Inference

Observed Evidence Inference Probability
Cloudy P(Rain | Cloudy) 75%
Rain P(Wet Grass | Rain) 91%
No Rain P(Wet Grass | No Rain) 16%

This demonstrates how a Bayesian Network can use observed evidence to determine the probability of another event.

13. Python Program 


# Practical 3: Simple Bayesian Network
# Cloudy -> Rain Sprinkle -> Wet Grass

# Prior Probability

P_cloudy = 0.60
P_not_cloudy = 0.40


# CPT for Rain Sprinkle

CPT_rain = {
    "Cloudy": {
        "Rain": 0.75,
        "No Rain": 0.25
    },

    "Not Cloudy": {
        "Rain": 0.25,
        "No Rain": 0.75
    }
}


# CPT for Wet Grass

CPT_grass = {
    "Rain": {
        "Wet Grass": 0.91,
        "Dry Grass": 0.09
    },

    "No Rain": {
        "Wet Grass": 0.16,
        "Dry Grass": 0.84
    }
}


# Display CPT for Rain

print("CPT for Rain Sprinkle")
print("----------------------")

for condition, probability in CPT_rain.items():

    print(condition)

    print("  P(Rain)     =", probability["Rain"])

    print("  P(No Rain)  =", probability["No Rain"])


# Display CPT for Wet Grass

print("\nCPT for Wet Grass")
print("-----------------")

for condition, probability in CPT_grass.items():

    print(condition)

    print("  P(Wet Grass) =", probability["Wet Grass"])

    print("  P(Dry Grass) =", probability["Dry Grass"])


# Inference 1: Cloudy -> Rain

evidence = "Cloudy"

print("\nEvidence:", evidence)

probability = CPT_rain[evidence]["Rain"]

print("P(Rain | Cloudy) =", probability)

print("Probability of Rain =", probability * 100, "%")


# Inference 2: Rain -> Wet Grass

evidence = "Rain"

print("\nEvidence:", evidence)

probability = CPT_grass[evidence]["Wet Grass"]

print("P(Wet Grass | Rain) =", probability)

print("Probability of Wet Grass =", probability * 100, "%")


# Inference 3: No Rain -> Wet Grass

evidence = "No Rain"

print("\nEvidence:", evidence)

probability = CPT_grass[evidence]["Wet Grass"]

print("P(Wet Grass | No Rain) =", probability)

print("Probability of Wet Grass =", probability * 100, "%")

14. Result

A simple Bayesian Network Cloudy → Rain Sprinkle → Wet Grass was successfully constructed. The Conditional Probability Tables (CPTs) were defined, and inference was performed using observed evidence.

The results show:

  • P(Rain | Cloudy) = 75%
  • P(Wet Grass | Rain) ≈ 91%
  • P(Wet Grass | No Rain) ≈ 16%

Thus, the practical demonstrates how a Bayesian Network represents probabilistic relationships and how observed evidence can be used to update probabilities, which is the central idea of Bayesian reasoning.

15. Conclusion

In this practical, we constructed a simple Bayesian Network consisting of Cloudy, Rain Sprinkle, and Wet Grass. We calculated prior probabilities, constructed Conditional Probability Tables (CPTs), and performed inference using observed evidence.

The practical demonstrates the important Bayesian reasoning process: 


Build the Network
        ↓
Define CPTs
        ↓
Observe Evidence
        ↓
Calculate Updated Probability

We learned that observing new evidence can change our belief about other variables. For example, knowing that it is cloudy gives a 75% probability of rain, while observing rain gives approximately a 91% probability of wet grass.
