Case Study 1 — Exploratory Analysis of Student Academic Performance

Scenario

A college has collected information about students, including study hours, attendance, assignment scores, internal assessment marks, and final examination marks. The college wants to understand the factors associated with student performance before developing a predictive model.

Sample Dataset

Student Study Hours Attendance (%) Assignment Score Internal Marks Final Marks
S1 2 65 55 50 52
S2 4 75 68 65 64
S3 5 82 75 72 76
S4 3 70 60 58 61
S5 6 90 85 82 88
S6 7 92 90 88 91
S7 1 55 45 42 40
S8 5 80 72 70 74

Task

Using Python and appropriate data-analysis libraries:

  1. Create a DataFrame containing the given data.
  2. Check the dataset for missing values.
  3. Calculate suitable descriptive statistics.
  4. Generate appropriate visualizations such as:
    • Bar chart
    • Histogram
    • Box plot
    • Scatter plot
  5. Study the relationship between study hours and final marks.
  6. Generate a correlation matrix and heatmap.
  7. Identify which variables appear to have a stronger relationship with final marks.
  8. Summarize the important findings.

Major Concepts

DataFrames, missing values, descriptive analytics, visualization, distribution, scatter plot, correlation, heatmap, exploratory data analysis.

Case Study 2 — Predicting House Prices Using Linear Regression

Scenario

A real-estate company wants to estimate the selling price of houses based on their characteristics. Historical data containing house area, number of bedrooms, age of the house, and distance from the city center are available.

Sample Dataset

House Area (sq.ft.) Bedrooms Age (Years) Distance (km) Price (₹ Lakh)
H1 800 2 15 12 35
H2 1000 2 10 10 45
H3 1200 3 8 8 55
H4 1500 3 5 6 70
H5 1800 4 4 5 85
H6 2000 4 3 4 95
H7 2200 4 2 3 110
H8 2500 5 1 2 125

Task

Develop a regression-based solution to predict house prices.

  1. Identify the dependent variable and independent variables.
  2. Explore the relationship between house area and price using a scatter plot.
  3. Build a Simple Linear Regression model using house area.
  4. Evaluate the model using suitable metrics such as:
    • R2
    • MAE
    • RMSE
  5. Plot the regression line and interpret the result.
  6. Extend the analysis using Multiple Linear Regression with more than one predictor.
  7. Compare the simple and multiple regression models.
  8. Discuss the factors that influence house prices.

Major Concepts

Simple Linear Regression, Multiple Linear Regression, model building, prediction, model diagnostics, R2, MAE, RMSE.

Case Study 3 — Credit Risk Classification

Scenario

A bank wants to develop a Machine Learning model that predicts whether a loan applicant is likely to be classified as a Low-Risk or High-Risk customer.

The dataset contains information such as income, credit score, loan amount, and previous payment history.

Sample Dataset

Applicant Income (₹'000) Credit Score Loan Amount (₹'000) Previous Default Risk
A1 35 580 500 Yes High
A2 60 720 400 No Low
A3 45 650 450 No Low
A4 30 550 600 Yes High
A5 80 760 500 No Low
A6 40 600 550 Yes High
A7 70 730 350 No Low
A8 50 620 500 No Low

Task

Develop a classification solution for predicting credit risk.

  1. Identify the target variable and predictor variables.
  2. Explain why this is a classification problem.
  3. Build a Binary Logistic Regression model.
  4. Divide the data into training and testing sets.
  5. Evaluate the model using appropriate measures such as:
    • Accuracy
    • Confusion matrix
    • Precision
    • Recall
  6. Explain the meaning of false positive and false negative in this banking scenario.
  7. Develop a Decision Tree Classification model.
  8. Compare the Logistic Regression and Decision Tree models.
  9. Discuss which model would be more appropriate for the bank and why.

Major Concepts

Classification, binary logistic regression, credit classification, confusion matrix, evaluation metrics, decision tree.

Case Study 4 — Customer Segmentation Using K-Means and Hierarchical Clustering

Scenario

A retail company wants to divide its customers into meaningful groups based on their annual income and annual spending score. The company intends to use these groups to design targeted marketing campaigns.

Sample Dataset

Customer Annual Income (₹'000) Spending Score
C1 25 20
C2 28 25
C3 30 22
C4 35 30
C5 70 75
C6 75 80
C7 80 78
C8 85 85
C9 45 50
C10 50 55

Task

Develop a customer segmentation solution.

  1. Explain why this is an unsupervised learning problem.
  2. Visualize the customer data using a scatter plot.
  3. Apply the K-Means clustering algorithm.
  4. Experiment with suitable values of K.
  5. Use an appropriate method, such as the Elbow Method, to help select the number of clusters.
  6. Visualize the resulting customer clusters.
  7. Interpret the characteristics of each cluster.
  8. Apply Hierarchical Clustering to the same data.
  9. Compare the clusters obtained using K-Means and Hierarchical Clustering.
  10. Recommend how the company could use the identified customer segments.

Major Concepts

Clustering, unsupervised learning, K-Means, cluster interpretation, Elbow Method, hierarchical clustering, product/customer segmentation.
