Practical No. 3

Exploratory Data Analysis: Summarizing Distributions and Visualizing Patterns and Relationships Using Data Visualization

1. Aim

To perform Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) on an academic dataset of college students by computing summary statistics and generating appropriate visualizations to understand how study habits, attendance, and internal assessment relate to examination performance.

2. Tools / Libraries

  • Python
  • Pandas
  • Matplotlib
  • Seaborn

The dataset used is a small Student Performance dataset containing 15 students from three departments. The dataset is created directly in Python so that the practical is fully reproducible in Jupyter Notebook or Google Colab.

3. Implementation

Step 1 — Create the Dataset and Inspect Its Structure

import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns

data = {
    'Department':     ['CS', 'CS', 'CS', 'CS', 'CS',
                       'IT', 'IT', 'IT', 'IT', 'IT',
                       'ECE', 'ECE', 'ECE', 'ECE', 'ECE'],

    'Study_Hours':    [2, 3, 5, 6, 8,
                       2, 4, 6, 7, 9,
                       3, 4, 5, 7, 8],

    'Attendance':     [65, 70, 85, 90, 95,
                       60, 75, 88, 92, 96,
                       68, 78, 82, 89, 93],

    'Internal_Marks': [18, 22, 30, 34, 38,
                       16, 26, 33, 36, 39,
                       20, 27, 29, 35, 37],

    'Final_Marks':    [55, 62, 78, 85, 92,
                       50, 68, 82, 88, 94,
                       60, 71, 76, 84, 90]
}

df = pd.DataFrame(data)

# Display the first five records
print(df.head())

# Display information about the dataset
print(df.info())

# Display the dimensions of the dataset
print(df.shape)

Output:

The dataset contains 15 rows and 5 columns. Department is a categorical variable, while Study_Hours, Attendance, Internal_Marks, and Final_Marks are numerical variables. The dataset contains no missing values.

Step 2 — Calculate Summary Statistics

# Display summary statistics for numerical variables
print(df.describe())

# Display the frequency of students in each department
print(df['Department'].value_counts())

Output:

The describe() function displays the count, mean, standard deviation, minimum, quartiles, and maximum values for each numerical variable.

For example, Study_Hours ranges from 2 to 9 hours, while Final_Marks range from 50 to 94 marks.

The value_counts() function shows that each department — CS, IT, and ECE — contains 5 students, resulting in a balanced sample.

Step 3 — Bar Chart for Categorical Frequency

sns.countplot(x='Department', data=df)

plt.title('Number of Students per Department')
plt.xlabel('Department')
plt.ylabel('Number of Students')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The bar chart contains three bars of equal height, with each department having 5 students. This confirms that the dataset is evenly distributed across CS, IT, and ECE.

Step 4 — Histogram for Distribution of Study Hours

sns.histplot(df['Study_Hours'], bins=6, kde=True)

plt.title('Distribution of Weekly Study Hours')
plt.xlabel('Study Hours per Week')
plt.ylabel('Frequency')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The histogram shows the frequency distribution of weekly study hours. The observations are distributed between 2 and 9 hours, without a strong concentration at either extreme. The KDE curve provides a smooth representation of the distribution.

Step 5 — Density Plot of Final Marks

sns.kdeplot(df['Final_Marks'], fill=True)

plt.title('Density Plot of Final Marks')
plt.xlabel('Final Marks')
plt.ylabel('Density')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The density plot represents the distribution of final marks. The distribution extends from 50 to 94 marks, with greater density toward the higher range of marks. This indicates that a larger proportion of students in this sample obtained moderately high to high final marks.

Step 6 — Box Plot for Final Marks

sns.boxplot(x=df['Final_Marks'])

plt.title('Box Plot of Final Marks')
plt.xlabel('Final Marks')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The box plot displays the median, interquartile range, and whiskers of the final marks. No individual observations appear beyond the whiskers, indicating that there are no apparent outliers in the final marks for this dataset.

Step 7 — Grouped Box Plot for Comparing Departments

sns.boxplot(x='Department', y='Final_Marks', data=df)

plt.title('Final Marks by Department')
plt.xlabel('Department')
plt.ylabel('Final Marks')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The grouped box plot compares the distribution of final marks across CS, IT, and ECE. The departments show some overlap in their performance ranges. This suggests that department alone does not provide a clear separation of student performance in this small sample.

Step 8 — Scatter Plot of Study Hours and Final Marks

sns.scatterplot(
    x='Study_Hours',
    y='Final_Marks',
    hue='Department',
    data=df
)

plt.title('Study Hours vs Final Marks')
plt.xlabel('Study Hours per Week')
plt.ylabel('Final Marks')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The scatter plot shows a clear positive relationship between study hours and final marks. In general, students who spend more hours studying tend to obtain higher final marks. The department-wise colours help compare the observations across the three departments.

Step 9 — Pair Plot for Pairwise Relationships

sns.pairplot(df, hue='Department')

plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The pair plot displays pairwise relationships among all numerical variables, with distributions shown along the diagonal. The plots indicate positive relationships among Study Hours, Attendance, Internal Marks, and Final Marks. The department-wise colours are also useful for observing whether the variables form distinct groups.

Step 10 — Correlation Matrix and Heatmap

# Calculate the correlation matrix
corr = df.drop('Department', axis=1).corr()

# Display the correlation heatmap
sns.heatmap(corr, annot=True, cmap='coolwarm', fmt='.2f')

plt.title('Correlation Heatmap of Student Variables')
plt.show()

Graph Interpretation:

The correlation heatmap displays the correlation coefficients among the four numerical variables. Internal_Marks and Final_Marks show a strong positive correlation. Study_Hours and Attendance also show positive relationships with Final_Marks.

Correlation analysis helps identify variables that may be useful for understanding and predicting academic performance.

5. Expected Outcome

After completing this practical, the student will be able to:

  1. Compute and interpret summary statistics for an academic dataset.
  2. Analyse the frequency distribution of categorical variables.
  3. Select appropriate plots for analysing distributions, comparisons, and relationships.
  4. Identify patterns, spread, and potential outliers using data visualizations.
  5. Examine relationships between study habits, attendance, internal marks, and final marks.
  6. Calculate and interpret correlations between numerical variables.
  7. Use Exploratory Data Analysis as a preliminary step for statistical analysis and machine learning.

6. Conclusion

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) helps transform raw academic data into meaningful numerical and visual insights. In this practical, a Student Performance dataset was analysed using Pandas, Matplotlib, and Seaborn. Summary statistics and various visualizations were used to examine department-wise distribution, study hours, final marks, outliers, and relationships among academic variables.

The analysis indicates a positive relationship between study hours, attendance, internal marks, and final marks in this sample. The practical demonstrates how EDA can be used as an important preliminary step before applying regression, classification, or other machine learning techniques.
